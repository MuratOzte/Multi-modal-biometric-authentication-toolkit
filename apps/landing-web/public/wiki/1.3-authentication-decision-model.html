Purpose and Scope

The Authentication Decision Model in SecureKit governs how multi-modal biometric signals (keystroke dynamics, face verification, voice recognition, and ID card OCR/matching) are combined with contextual risk indicators (IP geolocation, VPN/proxy/Tor detection) to evaluate user authenticity README.md 9-14 This page details the ingestion of signals, challenge-response lifecycles, consent recording, session tracking, and the risk evaluation logic that yields one of three terminal outcomes: allow, step_up, or deny README.md 14

1. Multi-Modal Signal Ingestion

SecureKit evaluates a heterogeneous set of biometric and contextual vectors to verify identity without relying strictly on static secrets:

  • Keystroke Dynamics: Captures precise keydown and keyup telemetry (dwell time and flight latency) via the web SDK. It evaluates both dynamic typing cadence against Exponential Moving Average (EMA) profiles and static challenge text verification README.md 9 MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 125-126
  • Face Verification: Processes frame captures through MTCNN and FaceNet pipelines to extract facial embeddings, perform liveness checks, and manage reference profiles via a sliding window mechanism README.md 10
  • Voice Verification: Ingests audio samples validated against random challenge phrases via Whisper for transcription accuracy and SpeechBrain for speaker similarity matching README.md 11
  • Card Verification: Combines document capture, PaddleOCR text extraction, and OpenCLIP visual similarity scoring to authenticate government-issued identification cards README.md 12
  • Network & IP Context: Queries IP intelligence services (such as VPNAPI) to flag VPNs, proxies, Tor exit nodes, data center traffic, and geolocation anomalies against defined country access policies README.md 13

Sources: README.md 7-14 MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 125-126

2. Challenge, Consent, and Session Management

SecureKit enforces cryptographic and stateful controls surrounding user interactions through dedicated storage managers and endpoints:

  • Challenge Generation and Consumption: Handled via POST /challenge/text and POST /challenge/text/consume. Challenges maintain a strict default Time-To-Live (TTL) of 120 seconds, enforce single-use consumption rules, and are managed in-memory via ChallengeStore MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 75-79
  • Consent Tracking: Recorded via POST /consent and persisted in file-based JSON storage utilizing atomic file writes and intra-process locking mechanisms to prevent race conditions MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 78-79
  • Session Lifecycle: Managed through POST /session/start and POST /verify/session. Sessions have a sliding expiration (defaulting to 15 minutes) tracked in the SessionStore MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 119-120 Signals across requests are accumulated, and historical keystroke metrics are updated against the user's EMA profile MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 121-126

Sources: MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 72-126

3. Risk Scoring and Decision Outcomes

The decision engine evaluates aggregated biometric deviation scores and contextual threat indicators through the SessionRiskEvaluator and supporting risk policies README.md 21 MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 121-124

Based on configurable risk weights, country-of-origin rules, and network threat flags, the evaluator outputs one of three explicit decisions:

  1. allow: The aggregate risk score falls below the primary threshold, and all mandatory biometric checks validate successfully. The session is authorized.
  2. step_up (or step-up): Moderate risk or partial biometric mismatch is detected. The session requires secondary verification (e.g., repeating a voice challenge or facial liveness check) before full access is granted MIGRATION_TO_ASPNET10_PLAN.md 122-123
  3. deny: High risk scores, blacklisted IP ranges, active Tor/proxy detection, or severe biometric verification failures trigger an immediate termination of the request or session.
